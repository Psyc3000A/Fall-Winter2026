## A. Treatment × Outcome (SPySS "Categorical data")

- **Chi-square size:** A chi-square test of independence showed a relation between treatment and outcome, χ²(1, N = 120) = 7.91, Cramér's V = .26, with more participants improving in the program group (83.3%) than in the usual-care group (58.3%).
- **p-value:** This relation was statistically significant, p = .005.
- **Residuals:** Standardized residuals showed fewer "not improved" cases than expected in the program group and more in usual care (±1.67), with the opposite pattern for "improved" (±1.07), though no residual exceeded |2|.

## B. NHANES: HowOftenDoYouFeelDepressed × EverToldDoctorHadTroubleSleeping

- **Chi-square size:** A chi-square test of independence showed a relation between how often participants felt depressed and whether they had ever told a doctor they had trouble sleeping, χ²(4, N = 5,436) = 482.61, Cramér's V = .30, with trouble sleeping rising from 17.0% at the lowest depression frequency to 64.7% at the highest.
- **p-value:** This relation was statistically significant, p < .001.
- **Residuals:** Standardized residuals showed that people at the higher depression frequencies reported trouble sleeping more often than expected (levels 2–4: 5.32 to 12.36), whereas those at the lowest frequency reported it less often than expected (−10.18), making these cells the strongest contributors (|residual| > 3).
